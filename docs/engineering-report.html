Security

Reviewed 4 Sep 2026 against the OWASP Mobile Top 10 surfaces that exist in a local-only app. The threat model is a single warden's phone holding tenant names, phone numbers, ID-proof photos and rent records; the attackers designed against are another app on the same device, and someone who picks the phone up. No code shipped since has touched an exported component, a permission, storage location or the PIN/crypto path, so this review's findings still describe the app as built.

Permissions actually shipped

The app declares one permission. The rest arrive through WorkManager and the biometric library in the merged manifest — the file that decides what the device grants.

PermissionOriginWhy it is not network access
POST_NOTIFICATIONSDeclared by LodgyRuntime-requested; degrades gracefully when denied
ACCESS_NETWORK_STATEWorkManagerReads connectivity state; cannot open a socket
WAKE_LOCK, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED, FOREGROUND_SERVICEWorkManagerScheduling only — the daily invoice, vacancy, dues and backup jobs
USE_BIOMETRIC, USE_FINGERPRINTAndroidX BiometricLocal unlock; no credential leaves the device
INTERNETAbsentWithout it the process cannot open a socket at all — the app is network-incapable, not merely network-abstinent

Reviewed and clean

  • One exported component. MainActivity, as a launcher requires. The FileProvider, WorkManager's initializer and the locale service are all exported="false". No receivers, no exported services, no custom permissions.
  • allowBackup="false". Stops adb backup lifting the unencrypted database and photos off the device.
  • PIN storage. bcrypt via BCrypt.gensalt() at cost 10, per-hash random salt. No fixed salt, no home-rolled hashing, no fast digest anywhere.
  • No logging of tenant data. No Log, println or printStackTrace anywhere in production source, so nothing reaches logcat.
  • Outbound intents are tap-to-send. ACTION_DIAL, wa.me and smsto: open another app pre-filled; nothing is dialled or transmitted automatically, and no CALL_PHONE or SEND_SMS permission is requested.

What UAT turned up. Every ticket through LODGY-104 went through user acceptance by someone working as the warden — onboarding, part payments, corrections, a checkout with a deposit deduction, a tenant-paid repair credited back, backup and restore. From LODGY-105 onward, the release shifted shape: most of it was built directly from specific, live requests rather than found in UAT, including six scope questions the developer raised explicitly rather than guessing (LODGY-110) — whether a property picker should update the app's shared context, whether two screens with similar filters should behave identically, and whether moving an expense between properties needed a confirmation. All were answered directly and built to match, rather than assumed.

Accepted limits

Recorded so that none of these reads as an oversight later. Each was considered, decided and written into the design.

  • The database is not encrypted at rest. The PIN gates the app, not the file. Extracting it requires root or a device image, which is out of scope for now; SQLCipher can be dropped in later without a schema change because it wraps the same Room database.
  • No historical occupancy for financial reporting. A monthly report for a past month uses today's bed states for occupancy, and says so on screen rather than implying otherwise (LODGY-52). This is separate from the tenant/bed occupancy history shipped in LODGY-91–95, which records who occupied what and when as real data - the report's occupancy percentage specifically still doesn't reconstruct a past month from it.
  • No automatic messaging. Reminders open WhatsApp or SMS pre-filled and stop; the warden reviews and sends. This is why no paid API, no backend and no SEND_SMS permission are needed.
  • No cloud sync and no account. Moving phones is the backup zip, by hand. Two people cannot share live data — that needs a real backend and is a phase-3 concern.
  • Branch coverage is not gated. Only line coverage is enforced. At 73% branch coverage, defensive early-returns are the least-tested paths in the codebase.
  • Google Drive is not integrated. Drive works because the system file picker offers it, and the app needs no Drive SDK, OAuth or network permission for that. The Drive-specific round trip was signed off by the product owner rather than demonstrated on a device with a Google account.
  • Personal/home expenses and income are out of scope. Every expense ties to a rentable property, and "income" only exists as tenant rent - there is no ledger for a warden's own household finances. Raised directly during this release and deliberately not started: it is closer to a product pivot (property tool → whole-life finance tool) than a fix, and needs its own scoping pass.
